One genius, or many minds? 🕵️

We sent AI to Victorian London to solve 200 murders three ways: a lone reasoning model (GPT6-Astra), Holmes (GPT6-Astra) leading a swarm of small Jev agents, and a leaderless Jev swarm.

🎬 Trailer + 📝 blog: [BLOG LINK]
[attach: the_irregulars_trailer.mp4]

**2/**
The setup: a seeded London of 100 addresses and 10 suspects. Each address holds one lead: a witness, a footprint, a red herring, or a lie.
To solve a case you must name the culprit, the motive AND the hideout within 12 case hours.

**3/**
Headline result over 100 cases:
• Holmes (GPT6-Astra) + Jev Swarm: 96 solved
• The Lone Genius (GPT6-Astra): 57
• Jev Swarm, no leader: 38
[attach: irregulars_results_table.png]

**4/**
Why does the genius lose? Legwork, not brains.
Alone it gets 20 visits and runs out of doors before finding the motive. Double its time and visits and it jumps to 91/100, but takes ~15 case hours. Holmes's 12 boys get to 96 in ~11 by searching in parallel.
[attach: irregulars_clip1_lone_genius.mp4]

**5/**
The winning split: Holmes (GPT6-Astra) never leaves 221B. Twelve Jev boys make the quick "where next?" calls, run back with what they find, and Holmes does the judging.
[attach: irregulars_clip2_holmes_and_the_irregulars.mp4]

**6/**
The leaderless swarm is fast: it reaches a 30-vote agreement in 91/100 cases, median 2.2 case hours, ~9 s real time.
But 53 of those 91 agreements are wrong. Agreeing is not the same as being right.
[attach: irregulars_clip3_swarm.mp4]

**7/**
How it fails: a few boys misjudge a clue, neighbours copy the confident vote, and the wrong answer snowballs to 30 before anyone checks. Small agents are good searchers and weak judges.

**8/**
Then we made somebody lie. In 100 "frame-up" cases the murderer bribes his servants to frame an innocent man, so the lie outnumbers the truth.
• Holmes (GPT6-Astra) + Jev Swarm: 93
• Lone Genius: 59
• Jev Swarm: 42
• Holmes that just counts clues: 0
[attach: irregulars_lies_table.png]

**9/**
On honest cases, a clue-counting Holmes with the same boys does fine (95/100, 20x faster). Under deception it names the framed man every time. Only the reasoning leader asks who is speaking, not how often.

**10/**
Takeaway: use swarms of small agents for coverage and speed, put a strong reasoner at the point of judgment. Holmes knew it in 1890.
