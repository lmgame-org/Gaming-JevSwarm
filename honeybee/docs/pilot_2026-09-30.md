# Honeybee pilot, 30 Sep 2026

Matched seeds 1-5, default settings (100 scouts, 5 sites, quorum 15 scouts at once), `python3 -m beeswarm.batch`.
"Best site" is how often the swarm lifted off to the highest-quality site (Wilson 95% interval).

| Scenario | Policy | Best site | Time to lift-off | Splits | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| classic | jev | 5/5 [0.57, 1.00] | 2.32 ± 0.79 h | 0 | $0.097 total, about $0.019 per run |
| classic | local_rule | 5/5 [0.57, 1.00] | 1.86 ± 0.35 h | 1 | free |
| classic | random | 3/5 [0.23, 0.88] | 3.54 ± 1.38 h | 0 | free |
| misinformation (15% believe the worst site is great) | jev | 3/5 [0.23, 0.88] | 1.51 ± 0.48 h | 0 | $0.067 total |
| misinformation | local_rule | 2/5 [0.12, 0.77] | 1.12 ± 0.22 h | 0 | free |
| misinformation | random | 2/5 [0.12, 0.77] | 1.85 ± 0.77 h | 0 | free |

With 40 seeds, the local rule picks the best site in 32/40 classic runs [0.65, 0.90].

Jev: 0 failures, 0 fallbacks, p50 latency about 0.17 s, about $0.00002 per call. Five seeds cannot separate the
policies; the intervals overlap everywhere. What the pilot does show is that misinformation makes the swarm decide
faster and less accurately for both Jev and the local rule. Next step: 20+ seeds per cell on close_call,
misinformation and noisy_overconfident (about $0.40 per 20 Jev runs).
